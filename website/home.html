Illustrative example

One word can move a threshold.

A fictional finding shows the handoff from language to decision. The actual skill runs in your assistant.

The draft and your instruction
Draft: “Vendor earns a $500 bonus when sales exceed $100,000.”

Your instruction: “We agreed the bonus starts at $100,000.”
A finding to review
Text: “exceed $100,000”
Issue: Exactly $100,000 does not satisfy this wording.
Effect: The threshold may differ from your stated deal.
Option: “equal or exceed $100,000.”
Decision: Confirm that the inclusive threshold was agreed.

Optional research module

Prediction needs outcome data.

An explicit opt-in offline experiment evaluates labeled, adjudicated enforcement outcomes. It reports cohort test metrics and a baseline. It does not score your contract, decide enforceability, or appear in the default review.

See the prediction boundary →
Future user-facing estimates require: a precisely defined event, representative reviewed examples, separate calibration and held-out testing, subgroup checks, and an “insufficient evidence” result. A model’s confidence in its answer is not a legal outcome probability.

Before you use it

Clear boundaries.

Does this site review my contract?

No. Download the skill and run it in a compatible assistant. This site has no contract upload.

Will it predict enforceability?

No. The optional offline research experiment evaluates labeled cohorts and cannot produce a validated probability for an individual agreement.

Does it edit the contract automatically?

Substantive edits require your recorded choice and a version-bound plan. Inspect every result; the gate cannot prove a legal conclusion.

Should an attorney review it?

Yes. If an attorney has not reviewed the complete agreement, seek attorney review before anyone signs.
